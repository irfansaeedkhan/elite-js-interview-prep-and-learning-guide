<TopicDefinition>

"Tell me about yourself" is not a request for your life story. It is a
test of whether you can tell a short, coherent story that ends at this
role.

Interviewers score three things. Can you pick the relevant parts of
your history. Can you finish in about a minute. And does the ending
make this role feel like the obvious next step rather than a random
application.

The trap is starting at the beginning. School, first job, every job
since, in order, until the interviewer stops listening. Your CV already
has the chronology. This answer needs the arc.

</TopicDefinition>

<TopicTab value="examples">

Here is what a strong answer sounds like. This is an illustrative
example, not your story. Swap in your own facts using the skeleton
below.

**Situation.** "I am a frontend engineer on a small product team, and
I own our checkout and billing pages in React and TypeScript."

**Task.** "For the last year my job has been turning a checkout that
people abandoned into one they finish, without breaking the billing
rules behind it."

**Action.** "I rebuilt the form validation so errors show next to the
field that caused them, added a review step before payment, and worked
with support to rewrite the three error messages customers complained
about most."

**Result.** "Checkout completion rose from 61 percent to 74 percent
over two months, and support tickets about payment errors fell by
about a third. That work is why I want a role where I own product
surfaces end to end, which is what this team does."

Notice what the answer does. It opens with the present, picks one past
beat that proves the skill this role needs, gives numbers, and lands
on the role. It takes about 75 seconds to say out loud.

</TopicTab>

<TopicTab value="indepth">

### STAR skeleton

Fill each line with your own facts before the interview, then practise
saying it out loud twice.

- **Present.** "I am a [role] working on [surface or product area]."
  One sentence. Your current work, not your title history.
- **Past.** "Before that I [one or two beats that built the skill
  this role needs]." Pick beats that match the job description. Leave
  out everything else.
- **Future.** "What I want next is [the work this role offers]."
  Name the work, not the perks. The role must feel like the next step,
  not an escape.

### Strong vs weak

Weak answer: "Well, I studied computer science, then I worked at a
startup for a year, then I moved to an agency, then I joined my
current company in 2023, where I do React and some backend, and I also
like design, and I am looking for new challenges."

Why it fails: it is a CV read aloud. No selection, no arc, no reason
this role follows from the story. The interviewer learns nothing they
could not have read.

Strong answer: "I am a frontend engineer, and most of my recent work
is building product UI in React and TypeScript. Before that I spent
two years at an agency shipping checkout flows, which is where I
learned to care about load time and form errors. What I want next is
to own product surfaces end to end, and this role pairs that ownership
with a team that treats accessibility as real work."

Why it works: three beats, each one earns its place. The past proves a
skill, the future names the role's actual work, and the whole thing
fits in a minute.

### Traps

- Starting with school or childhood. Start with the present.
- Listing every job. Two past beats at most.
- Ending on "I am looking for new challenges." End on the work you
  want, named specifically.
- Going past 90 seconds. Time yourself. If you cannot finish it in a
  minute and a half, cut a beat.

### Follow-up prep

- "Why are you leaving your current role?" Answer with what you are
  moving toward, not what you are escaping. One sentence on the pull,
  none on the grievances.
- "What kind of work do you want more of?" Name the same work you
  named in your future beat. Consistency is the point.
- "What drew you to this company specifically?" Name one real thing
  about the product or team, not the brand. If you cannot name one,
  research before the interview.
