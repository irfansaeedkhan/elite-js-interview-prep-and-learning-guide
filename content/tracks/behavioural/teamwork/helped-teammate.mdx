<TopicDefinition>

"Tell me about a time you helped a teammate who was struggling"
tests empathy plus judgement. Anyone can fix a bug for someone. The
question is whether you helped in a way that left the person stronger.

Interviewers score three things. Did you notice the struggle, since
struggling teammates often go quiet. Did you help in a way that kept
their ownership. And did the person grow, not just the ticket move.

The trap is the rescue story. "I took over the ticket and shipped it
myself" helps the project and hurts the teammate. It also tells the
panel you will do the same to them.

</TopicDefinition>

<TopicTab value="examples">

Here is what a strong answer sounds like. This is an illustrative
example, not your story. Swap in your own facts using the skeleton
below.

**Situation.** "A junior teammate had been stuck on a date handling
bug for two days. His updates got shorter, which I recognised as a
sign he was stuck, not finished."

**Task.** "I needed to unblock him without taking the ticket, because
he owned that area and would own the next bug in it too."

**Action.** "I asked him to walk me through what he had already
ruled out, instead of asking what the bug was. That showed me he had
tested the display code but not the conversion step. We found the
timezone conversion was happening twice. I asked him to write the
failing test first, then pointed at the conversion function and let
him make the fix. The whole thing took forty minutes."

**Result.** "He shipped it that day. A month later he caught the same
class of bug in someone else's review and explained it to them the
same way, test first. The help stuck because he did the fix himself."

The answer works because the help has a method (rule-out walkthrough,
test first) and the result is about the teammate's growth, not the
speaker's heroics.

</TopicTab>

<TopicTab value="indepth">

### STAR skeleton

- **Situation.** "A teammate was struggling with [task], and I
  noticed because [signal]." The signal matters. It shows you pay
  attention to people, not just code.
- **Task.** "I needed to help without [taking over / embarrassing
  them / breaking their ownership]." Name the constraint.
- **Action.** "I [the method: a question, a walkthrough, a pairing
  session, a test first]." The method is the answer. "I helped him"
  is not.
- **Result.** "They [shipped it / learned the method / later helped
  someone else]." A result about the teammate beats a result about
  the project.

### Strong vs weak

Weak answer: "A teammate was stuck on a bug, so I took the ticket and
fixed it myself that afternoon. The project stayed on track and he was
really grateful."

Why it fails: the teammate learned nothing, lost ownership of their
work, and the speaker's help is really a takeover. Panels read this
as a red flag, not generosity.

Strong answer: "A junior teammate was stuck on a bug in our date
handling for two days and was going quiet about it. I asked him to
walk me through what he had ruled out, and we found the issue was a
timezone conversion happening twice. Instead of fixing it for him, I
asked him to write the failing test first, then guided him to the
fix."

Why it works: the noticing is explicit (going quiet), the method is
concrete (rule-out walkthrough, test first), and the teammate keeps
the fix and the credit.

### Traps

- The takeover disguised as help. If your answer ends with you
  shipping their work, rewrite it.
- Vague noticing. "I could tell he was struggling" needs the signal.
  Shorter updates, missed stand-ups, a ticket not moving.
- Helping only when asked. The best version of this story starts
  with you noticing, not with them asking.
- No result for the person. "The ticket moved" is a project result.
  The question asks about a person.

### Follow-up prep

- "How do you know when to step in versus let someone struggle?"
  Have a rule ready. Two days stuck, or silence replacing updates,
  are concrete triggers.
- "Have you ever been the one who needed help?" Say yes, with a
  short story. Denying it sounds arrogant.
- "How do you give feedback without embarrassing someone?" Private
  first, specific second, behaviour third. Never in a group channel.
