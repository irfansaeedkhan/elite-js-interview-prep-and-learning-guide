<TopicDefinition>

"Tell me about a time you delivered under a tight deadline" tests
prioritisation under pressure, not endurance. Interviewers do not
want to hear that you worked all night. They want to hear what you
cut, what you protected, and who agreed to the trade-off.

They score three things. Did you make the scope decision explicitly,
instead of silently dropping quality. Did you communicate it before
the deadline. And did the thing that mattered actually work.

The trap is the heroics story. "I worked the weekend and we shipped
everything" sounds dedicated and teaches the panel nothing about
judgement. It also suggests you will burn out rather than negotiate.

</TopicDefinition>

<TopicTab value="examples">

Here is what a strong answer sounds like. This is an illustrative
example, not your story. Swap in your own facts using the skeleton
below.

**Situation.** "We had ten days to ship a new pricing page before a
conference, and the design arrived four days late. That left six days
for a page planned for ten."

**Task.** "I owned the frontend, and the non-negotiable part was the
price calculator and the checkout link. Everything else was decoration
by comparison."

**Action.** "I proposed cutting the comparison table and the
animated transitions, and I wrote the cut list down and got the
product manager to agree to it in writing before we started. That
mattered, because it stopped the cut pieces creeping back in during
the week. I also set a daily fifteen minute check-in so a slip would
surface the same day."

**Result.** "We shipped on the conference morning. The calculator
worked for every plan, the checkout link worked, and nobody at the
conference asked about the missing table. The cut pieces shipped the
following week, planned, not as a panic fix."

The answer works because the scope cut is explicit, agreed in
writing, and the protected core is named. The heroics are absent,
which is the point.

</TopicTab>

<TopicTab value="indepth">

### STAR skeleton

- **Situation.** "We had [time] to ship [thing], and [what went
  wrong with the plan]." State the compression honestly.
- **Task.** "The non-negotiable part was [core]. Everything else
  was [cuttable]." If you cannot name the core, you did not
  prioritise.
- **Action.** "I proposed cutting [list], got [decision maker] to
  agree, and [how you kept the week visible]." Agreement in writing
  beats agreement in a chat that scrolls away.
- **Result.** "We shipped on [date]. [Core] worked, and [cut pieces]
  shipped [when, planned]." A planned follow-up beats a forgotten cut.

### Strong vs weak

Weak answer: "We had a really tight deadline for a pricing page, so
I worked late every night that week and we managed to ship everything
on time. It was stressful but the team pulled together."

Why it fails: no scope decision, no protected core, no communication.
It says the deadline was met by effort alone, which is not a repeatable
skill and not a story a senior panel respects.

Strong answer: "We had ten days to ship a new pricing page before a
conference, and the design arrived four days late. I proposed cutting
the comparison table and the animated transitions, but protecting the
price calculator and the checkout link, and I got the product manager
to agree in writing. We shipped on the conference morning, the
calculator worked for every plan, and the cut pieces shipped the
following week."

Why it works: the trade-off is explicit, agreed before building, and
the result separates what shipped from what was deferred. That is
prioritisation, not endurance.

### Traps

- Heroics without decisions. Effort is not a strategy.
- Silent quality cuts. Shipping broken work on time is worse than
  shipping less, and panels know it.
- Blaming the late design. State the delay factually, then move to
  your decision. The story is about your response.
- No follow-up for the cut work. Deferred work with no plan is just
  dropped work.

### Follow-up prep

- "How do you decide what to cut when time runs out?" Have a rule:
  protect the path that makes the user succeed, cut everything that
  only makes it prettier.
- "Have you ever missed a deadline? What happened?" Say yes if true,
  say what you communicated early, and what changed afterwards.
- "How do you push back on an unrealistic deadline?" Show one
  negotiation: the trade-off you proposed, not just the refusal.
