<TopicDefinition>

"Walk me through your CV" is the same question as "Tell me about
yourself" with the document in front of the interviewer. They will
follow your CV line by line, so your story must survive contact with
it.

Interviewers score three things. Does the story match the document,
with no surprises. Do transitions make sense in one sentence each.
And does the walk end at this role, not just at your last job.

The trap is narrating every bullet. Your CV has the detail. This
answer needs one story per role, chosen for this job, plus honest
one-liners for transitions and gaps. Anything longer loses the room.

</TopicDefinition>

<TopicTab value="examples">

Here is what a strong walkthrough sounds like. This is an
illustrative example, not your story. Swap in your own facts using
the template below.

**Situation.** "I started at an agency building marketing sites,
where I learned to care about load time because clients measured it
in the contract."

**Task.** "After two years I wanted to own surfaces end to end
instead of handing sites over, so I moved to a product team."

**Action.** "There I spent two years on checkout and billing. The
main piece of work was rebuilding form validation, which took
checkout completion from 61 percent to 74 percent. I took three
months between those roles to care for a family member, and I used
part of that time to study accessibility properly, which is how it
became a real interest rather than a line on my CV."

**Result.** "What I want next is a team where I own product surfaces
and accessibility is part of done, which is why I am here."

Notice the structure. Two roles, one story each, chosen because they
lead to this job. The gap gets one plain sentence, no apology, no
rambling. The ending names this role. The whole walk takes about two
minutes, which is the right length for a CV with two or three roles.

</TopicTab>

<TopicTab value="indepth">

### CV walkthrough template

Prepare one block per role before the interview, then say only the
blocks that lead to this job.

- **Role line.** "At [company] I [owned surface or area], and the
  work that matters for this role was [one piece]." One piece per
  role, not the whole job description.
- **Transition line.** "I moved because [one honest reason]." One
  sentence. Reasons that work: wanted ownership, wanted product
  work, wanted a different problem. Reasons that fail: vague
  complaints about a former employer.
- **Gap line.** "I took [time] to [plain reason], and I [one useful
  thing from that time]." Plain, brief, then move on. Gaps only grow
  when you defend them.
- **Ending line.** "What I want next is [the work this role
  offers]." The walk must end at this job, not at your last job.

### Strong vs weak

Weak answer: "So I started at company A in 2021 where I did React
and TypeScript and some Node, then I moved to company B in 2023 where
I did similar work plus Next.js, and then there was a gap, well, I
was looking for work, and now I am at company C doing frontend."

Why it fails: it lists employers and stacks, tells no story, explains
no transition, and apologises for the gap by rambling. The panel
learns nothing except the dates they already had.

Strong answer: "I started at an agency building marketing sites,
where I learned to care about load time because clients measured it.
Then I moved to a product team to own surfaces end to end, and spent
the last two years on checkout and billing, where completion rose
from 61 percent to 74 percent after a validation rebuild."

Why it works: each role contributes one story beat, the transition
has a reason (ownership), and the number gives the panel something
to ask about. The story selects; the CV lists.

### Drill-down questions to prepare

Interviewers follow the walk with questions about specific lines.
Prepare one line each for:

- Every role change. One honest reason, no complaints.
- Every gap longer than a month. One plain sentence, then move on.
- Every claim with a number. Know the before and after, and what
  you personally did versus what the team did.
- Every technology listed. One project where you used it properly,
  not just touched it.
- The oldest role. One sentence is enough. Panels care about recent
  work; do not spend two minutes on a job from five years ago.

### Traps

- Reading the CV aloud. They have it. Tell the story behind it.
- Apologising for gaps or short roles. State the fact, state the
  reason, move on. Apology invites more questions.
- Badmouthing a former employer. One line about what you wanted
  next beats any complaint about what you left.
- Ending on your last job. Always end on this role and the work you
  want next.

### Follow-up prep

- "Why did you leave your last role?" Same as your transition line.
  Consistency matters more than novelty.
- "Tell me about the gap in your CV." Your gap line, word for word
  if needed. Short and plain.
- "Which role taught you the most, and why?" Pick the role whose
  lesson matches this job, and name the lesson as a habit, not a
  moral.
